Methodology

Where every number comes from, what it can and can't tell you, and how the videos were chosen.

The short version. Box scores and play-by-play show what happened while two players were on the floor together. They don't show who was guarding whom on each possession, so every number here is evidence about Gobert's defense, not a direct measurement of it.

Which games count

A game counts if it appears in both players' ESPN game logs, so games where either one sat out are excluded. That includes regular-season and playoff games (and the play-in), but not preseason. Wembanyama's games start in 2023-24; Durant's start with Gobert's rookie season, 2013-14.

Head-to-head lines

The opponent's points, shooting and turnovers come from ESPN box scores. "His other games" is his average in every other game of the same season(s), so a season where he faced Gobert once is compared with that season, not his whole career. True shooting = points ÷ (2 × (field-goal attempts + 0.44 × free-throw attempts)), a single efficiency number that counts threes and free throws.

Gobert on vs. on the bench

ESPN play-by-play logs substitutions but not who starts each quarter, so lineups are rebuilt:

  • Starters come from the box score.
  • After the first quarter, a player is assumed on the court at the start of a quarter if his first event in that quarter is anything other than checking in.
  • Older ESPN data sometimes skips the substitution when a player sits out a whole quarter (common in blowouts). For quarters where a player has no events at all, the build tests both possibilities and keeps whichever matches his official minutes.
  • Every rebuilt total is checked against the box score: the opponent's points must match, and both players' minutes must be within 2 minutes. In the current build

Only time when the opponent was on the floor counts. Splits with under minutes are flagged as small samples. The main limitation: when Gobert rests, the other team often rests its starters too, so "Gobert off" minutes can mean easier or harder competition for reasons that have nothing to do with him.

Shots and blocks

Shot locations are ESPN's play-by-play coordinates. "At the rim" means 4 feet or closer by ESPN's stated distance, or a layup or dunk with no distance given. A block counts when ESPN's play lists Gobert as the blocker of the opponent's shot.

NBA.com tracking (optional)

NBA.com's matchup data uses player tracking to credit each possession's points to a primary defender. It's the closest public measure of "Gobert guarding him", but it's only available from 2017-18 on and can't be downloaded automatically from this project. The section appears only when that data has been added (see the README for how to export it).

Videos

  • Candidates were found by web search, then checked with YouTube's public embed lookup, which confirms the video exists, allows embedding, and reports its real title and channel.
  • Only official league, team and network channels are kept, and the title must name Gobert. Fan re-uploads of broadcast footage are left out even when relevant.
  • Clips are chosen from titles and descriptions; they haven't been reviewed frame by frame.
  • Videos are embedded from YouTube (privacy-enhanced mode, loaded only when you press play) and are never downloaded or re-hosted.
